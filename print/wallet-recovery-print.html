Free Lost Wallet Recovery Kit

For the worst Tuesday of the month. Plain English, step by step, never condescending. No affiliate links, nothing to sign up for.

The panic script: “My wallet is missing. I am calling my banks, in order of damage risk. I freeze first and figure out the rest later. Freezing is free. Phone numbers come from bank websites and apps, not from memory.”

Part 1 — What-to-Do Checklist

The first 30 minutes

  • Retrace, briefly. Pockets, car, last three places visited, the couch. Ten minutes, no more, right now.
  • Stolen or lost? Charges appearing, or phone also gone → treat as theft (see Part 4).
  • Check your two most-used card apps for charges you didn’t make.
  • Pick your word: “lost” or “stolen.” Unsure? Say “lost or possibly stolen” and stay consistent.

The first hour — freeze everything

  • Call each debit card’s bank first — freeze or cancel (debit is riskier).
  • Call each credit card issuer — freeze, or cancel-and-replace.
  • Freeze your credit: Equifax, TransUnion — and place a fraud alert at Experian (one bureau notifies all three).
  • Ask your bank to disable phone-wallet (Apple Pay / Google Pay) tokens for the lost cards.
  • If any passwords were written down in the wallet, change those accounts now.

The rest of the first day

  • Update saved cards in stores/subscription apps; remove the dead card.
  • List everything on autopay that charged a vanished card (Part 3 has rows for this).
  • File a police report if stolen, ID gone, or a bank asks for a report number (Part 4).
  • If work badges/HSA/parking cards were inside, tell your employer.
  • Email yourself a short note: date noticed, what was inside, who you've called.

Days 2–7

  • Order replacements, one row at a time from the tracker (Part 3). License and debit first.
  • Scan every account once a day for odd charges; fraud can wait weeks to show up.
  • Turn on transaction alerts for all replaced cards.
  • Stolen license? Consider a free FTC report at IdentityTheft.gov.

Week 2 and after

  • Send dispute letters if needed (Part 5). Follow up at 10 business days if silent.
  • Confirm every tracker row arrived.
  • Do the 30-minute prevention sheet (Part 6). Once. Then relax.

Part 5 — Fraud / Dispute Note Templates

Letter 1: to the card issuer (unauthorized charge dispute)

Subject: Unauthorized charge dispute — card ending [XXXX]

Dear [Bank / Issuer],

I am disputing a charge of $[XX.XX] to [MERCHANT AS SHOWN] on [MM/DD/YYYY] on the account above. I did not make, authorize, or benefit from this charge. Card ending [XXXX] was lost on or about [DATE]; I reported it on [DATE], your reference number [CONFIRMATION #]. [Optional: police report number [REPORT #], filed [DATE].]

I ask that the charge be removed per your fraud and zero-liability policies, and that a written confirmation of the outcome be sent to me.

Sincerely, [Your name] · [Daytime phone]

Send it from the bank’s own app or website (secure message / dispute-a-charge window) whenever possible — it timestamps everything. Mail is the backup.

Letter 2: to anyone else (employer badge, insurer, subscription, DMV)

Subject: Wallet lost on [DATE] — account [last 4]

Hello,

My wallet was lost or possibly stolen on or about [DATE]; it contained [ITEM], which affects this account. I have already reported the cards missing to my banks on [DATE] and placed fraud alerts/credit freezes on [DATE]. [Optional: police report number [___].]

Please: [replace the card/badge on this account], [confirm when the replacement arrives], and [flag the account for unusual activity].

Thank you, [Your name]
  • Dispute within 2 business days when you can — protection is strongest early.
  • Ask for a dispute confirmation number every time and log it in the call list.
  • Keep it in writing where possible; follow up by phone + secure message is ideal.
  • No answer at 10 business days? That’s the normal moment to check in.

Part 6 — Prevention Sheet (for after)

Thirty minutes, one time, nothing leaves your house.

1. Slim the wallet

  • Wallet keeps: one debit card, one credit card, license, transit card if used.
  • Drawer keeps: spare cards, Social Security card, chequebook, rarely-used cards, anything with name + address + photo together.

2. Make the vault

  • Write a wallet-inventory card (below) and keep it at home — never in the wallet.
  • Photograph wallet contents front and back — cover or crop the CVV. Private album on your own device only.
  • Rule adopted: keys never live in the wallet.

3. Phone wallet + alerts

  • One card in Apple/Google/Samsung Pay — you can pay even with a lost wallet.
  • Push alerts on for every transaction on every card: this is the alert that catches it in aisle 4 instead of day 3.
